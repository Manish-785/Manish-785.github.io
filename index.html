About

I am a final-year B.Tech. student in Chemical Engineering at IIT Bombay, with a minor in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science. My main project, with Prof. Sudarshan Vijay, is an optimiser that uses curvature from a machine-learned interatomic potential to find reaction transition states with far fewer force evaluations than the standard method. Finding transition states with machine-learned curvature

Ongoing · B.Tech. project with Prof. Sudarshan Vijay, IIT Bombay · since May 2025

  • Nudged elastic band
  • MACE-MP-0
  • Hessian preconditioning
  • Safeguarded L-BFGS

The nudged elastic band (NEB) method finds the lowest-energy path of a reaction and its transition state, but each step needs expensive force calls, usually from DFT. I precondition the optimiser with the Hessian of a machine-learned potential (MACE-MP-0): projected onto the directions perpendicular to the path and paired with an analytical spring-force Jacobian along it, inside a safeguarded L-BFGS. Getting this to work meant diagnosing why curvature-based preconditioning breaks down near a saddle point, where the Hessian is indefinite and the two subspaces leak into each other.

The hot-season peak in maternal and newborn deaths in India

Manuscript in preparation · R&D project with Prof. Saket Choudhary, Koita Centre for Digital Health, IIT Bombay

  • Poisson fixed-effects models
  • ERA5 climate data
  • NFHS-5
  • 772 districts, 2009–2024

Births in India are seasonal, but deaths of mothers and newborns follow a different calendar. Using district-month health records for 772 districts from 2009 to 2024, linked to ERA5 climate and NFHS-5 survey data, I show that deaths per birth peak every year in May–July, the hottest months. Poisson models with district-by-year fixed effects compare each district only with itself; the peak survives five different birth registers as denominators, and the neonatal peak appears independently in the national household survey (NFHS-5).

Spatiotemporal graph neural networks for multi-store forecasting

Preprint · Course project, EE782, with Prof. Amit Sethi · with Arpita Dayama · Aug–Nov 2025

  • Spatiotemporal GNN
  • Learned adjacency
  • Dilated temporal convolutions
  • ARIMA · XGBoost · LSTM baselines

Retail stores share trends, so forecasting them one at a time throws information away. We forecast weekly sales for 45 Walmart stores jointly with a spatiotemporal GNN that learns its own store-to-store graph, combining dilated temporal convolutions with graph convolution, and compared it with ARIMA, XGBoost and LSTM under identical features and splits. The learned graph recovered functional groups of stores without any location data.

Quant Auditor: a small language model that audits trading backtests

Ongoing · Course project, IE624 Generative and Agentic AI · team of two, with Arpita

  • LLM agents
  • Qwen3-4B fine-tuning
  • Matched-pair training data

A trading strategy can look profitable only because of hidden mistakes such as look-ahead, ignored trading costs or in-sample tuning. I am building an auditor agent that investigates strategy code by running experiments on it, with one rule: no number reaches the user unless it is cited to its source or produced by an executed test. It is trained on flawed backtests paired with clean controls that differ in exactly one detail, using a fine-tuned Qwen3-4B on free GPUs.

Generative AI for drug discovery

1st of 64,000+ participants · Google Solution Challenge 2025 · team project

  • BiLSTM with attention
  • BindingDB (550K+)
  • DiffDock
  • MolFormer

A protein-conditioned recurrent network generates candidate molecules for a given target: a bidirectional LSTM with attention encodes the protein, and the generator is trained on 550K+ BindingDB samples. Candidates are ranked on drug-likeness, toxicity, solubility and predicted binding, using DiffDock and MolFormer.
